Raleigh-Durham: A Local's Guide to the Triangle

Parks, food, desserts, activities, and neighborhoods worth knowing in the Raleigh-Durham area — whether you're visiting, showing guests around, or just exploring your own backyard.

A running guide to the Triangle — built from what we keep coming back to and what we take every guest to first.

Parks & Outdoors

The red bridge at Sarah P. Duke Gardens, Durham · Photo by Drew Easley on Unsplash

  • Duke Gardens (Durham) — the first place we take every guest. Lush, beautifully maintained, and bigger than it looks. There's a staircase of layered plants, reflecting ponds, a red bridge, and a Japanese garden section that genuinely feels like you've left North Carolina. Finding parking takes a minute but once you're inside it's worth every bit of it. Bring a book, lay out a blanket, do a picnic — it's that kind of place.
  • William B. Umstead State Park (Cary/Raleigh) — the drive in already sets the mood. Miles of trails ranging from easy walks to longer loops, all surrounded by forest. A great way to reset. Trails connect into a broader network stretching across Durham and Raleigh.
  • Dorothea Dix Park (Raleigh) — a former hospital campus that the city transformed beautifully. Great for families: there's a kids' waterfall play area, a sunflower field in season, a jumping bridge, and clean restrooms throughout. Lots of open space for whatever pace you're in.
  • Downtown Cary Park (Cary) — one of the better park transformations in the area. The lotus pond is the signature feature; add in the amphitheater, dog park, food trucks during events, fire features, seating areas, and it makes for a great date or family afternoon. Architecture around the park is worth taking in too.
  • Neuse River Trail & Greenways — if you like running, biking, or just long walks, the greenway network across Raleigh and Durham is extensive. The Neuse River Trail is one of the longer options and easy to access from multiple points.

Food

Thai noodles · Photo by 8-Low Ural on Unsplash

  • Champa Thai (Durham) — our most reliable recommendation in the area. Every time, never misses. Go here first.
  • Ramen Boulevard — if you like ramen that's thick, creamy, and leans closer to Thai curry than traditional Japanese broth, try the yellow curry ramen. We always get it with vegetable and tofu. Hits every time.
  • BJ's Restaurant & Brewhouse — more underrated than it should be. Get the pizza, wings, and a Pizookie to close it out. Solid, satisfying meal every visit.
  • Indian (Southern style) — there are several Indian restaurants across Raleigh and Durham; look for one that skews South Indian if that's your preference. The flavors run different from North Indian, and there are a few good options worth finding depending on your neighborhood.
  • Italian — both Raleigh and Durham have hidden Italian gems worth tracking down. We'll keep updating this list as we find new ones.

Desserts & Boba

  • Kilwins (North Hills, Raleigh) — chocolate shop and ice cream. The chocolate-covered apples are the standout — they make them fresh, reportedly on Fridays, so time your visit if you can. Chocolate-covered nuts and fudge also worth picking up.
  • Meet Fresh — Taiwanese dessert and boba. Located near North Raleigh. Everything we've had has been good, every time. If you're a boba person this is the one to try.
  • Fenton (dessert options) — the shops at Fenton carry dessert options worth exploring after a walkthrough. More on Fenton in the Neighborhoods section.

Activities

  • TopGolf — book a bay in advance and make sure you have a card or account set up before you arrive; walk-ins can mean a wait. Good for groups, couples, or a casual solo session.
  • Go-Kart (near TopGolf) — there's a go-kart venue that's been gaining attention near the TopGolf location. We haven't tried it yet but it's on the list — check what's current in the area.
  • Escape Rooms — a good one for friend groups or visiting guests. You come out knowing each other a little better, and there's a real satisfaction in solving it together. Several options available across Raleigh and Durham; book in advance on weekends.
  • Pickleball & Tennis — courts available in Morrisville and Sanford, among other locations. They do get busy, especially evenings and weekends. One of the better parts of pickleball: if a court needs two more players, you can just join an ongoing game.
  • Movies at Fenton — the theater at Fenton has private pod-style seating. A step up from a standard movie night.

Neighborhoods & Events

Raleigh skyline from Boylan Bridge · Photo by Elijah Mears on Unsplash

  • Fenton (Cary) — a mixed-use development that feels upscale without being intimidating. Good for a slow afternoon: walk the space, window shop, grab a bite, catch a movie. There are restaurants, boutiques, a movie theater, and generally a calm energy. Works well for families, dates, or hosting out-of-town guests who want somewhere polished to spend a few hours.
  • Downtown Raleigh — check the city events calendar before writing it off as quiet. Both Raleigh and Durham run regular events, markets, and outdoor programming. Sometimes the calendar is thin; sometimes there's something worth building a day around.
  • Downtown Durham — similar energy to Raleigh's downtown but with more of an arts and independent restaurant footprint. Worth checking what's on when you go.

This is a living list — we'll keep adding as we find new spots.
