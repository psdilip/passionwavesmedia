I came into Customer Success Manager (CSM) after years in infrastructure and DevOps. It was a career growth move, and a test of whether I could thrive in a public-facing, customer-centric role. Two-plus years in, here's what actually made the difference: the attributes, systems, and habits I wish someone had handed me on day one.

I worked at a full-service IT consulting company — everything from cloud to AI to data — so a lot of this is shaped by that environment, but the core lessons transfer anywhere you own the customer relationship.

### Core attributes

**Adaptability** is the bare minimum, especially in tech. New technologies show up constantly, and you need to understand them deeply enough to explain them, not just repeat a pitch.

- Learn new technologies quickly, down to their dependencies and intricacies
- Understand your own product well enough to align it with what the customer actually needs
- Translate customer requirements into internal, actionable tasks

**Communication** is the backbone of the role, internally and externally.

- Pick a consistent channel (email, Slack, Teams) and stick to it
- Know when email won't cut it: escalate to a call or meeting
- Follow up relentlessly; this is the real differentiator
- Document everything
- Run regular business reviews

**Ownership and coordination.** You're the internal point person making things happen: the one talking to legal, FinOps, sales, technical account managers, industry leads, and billing, all to get one contract out the door and supported through its life.

- Own the workflow from opportunity → contract → delivery → renewal
- Get clear requirements from the customer, then hold your internal team to exactly what was promised
- Know who to go to for what, and who covers for whom when someone's out

**Customer trust** is the actual goal underneath everything else.

- Deliver exactly what you promised
- Respond quickly
- Bring technical clarity, not just status updates
- Make the customer feel heard and safe about their investment in you

### Build the systems nobody hands you

Most teams won't have this dressed up for you — you build it.

- Contract and CRM tracking (if one doesn't exist, implement it)
- Customer spend dashboards
- Ticketing workflows and renewal calendars
- A knowledge base per customer, and a reusable template for business reviews
- If something works well for one customer, turn it into your golden template and share it with the team

**Automate what you can.** Most of your day goes to follow-ups, ticket responses, and outreach: automate the repeatable parts (cadence reminders, cost reports, feature updates) and use AI to research, summarize new features, and get up to speed on unfamiliar tech fast.

### Prepare for every meeting like it's the differentiator — because it is

Go beyond what the customer asked for:

- Branded deck with their cost consumption broken down by service
- New features or documentation relevant to them
- Cost optimization strategies (instance types, pricing models, what the provider offers to cut spend)
- Anything new from the hyperscaler or recent conferences
- Progress against their stated goals, current obstacles, and concrete next steps
- Bring in a subject matter expert when the conversation needs one

### Master the internal workflow

Every contract touches contract/legal, your manager, industry lead, sales, technical account managers, bid desk, FinOps, and billing. Streamlining this is your job, not something you wait for someone else to fix.

- Know who approves what, who unblocks what, and who's covering during PTO
- Store documents centrally so anyone can answer a question fast
- Work with your manager to reduce approval friction: time-to-market on a contract is a real differentiator for the customer
- Help sales translate technical requirements so the right thing actually gets sold

### Stay organized

- Build your own dashboard: communication history, contract expiration dates, renewal timelines, ticket status
- Plan around time zones: approvals in London, ticketing in Asia, and so on
- Prioritize customers deliberately: contract value, strategic importance, growth potential, urgency, not just whoever emailed last

### Day-to-day principles

- Find the right way to communicate with each customer: don't force everyone through the same channel
- Overdeliver
- Lead with confidence; prepare enough that you never walk in unsure
- How you show up matters
- Ask questions
- Prioritize ruthlessly, and offload when you have to
- Stay unified with your team: share what works instead of hoarding it

### The bottom line

Being a great CSM comes down to adaptability, communication, ownership, and relentless organization, in service of one goal: the customer trusts you with their investment. Build the systems, overprepare for every conversation, and the rest follows.

## Practical guide: a checklist to actually run this

A condensed version of everything above, distilled into what to actually do.

1. **Pick one primary channel per customer and stick to it.** Use email, Slack, or Teams consistently, but know when a message needs to become a call or meeting instead.
2. **Follow up relentlessly and document everything.** This is the real differentiator between an average CSM and a great one, and it feeds every business review you'll ever run.
3. **Build the systems nobody hands you.** Set up contract and CRM tracking, a customer spend dashboard, ticketing workflows with renewal calendars, and a knowledge base per customer if they don't already exist.
4. **Automate the repeatable parts of your day.** Cadence reminders, cost reports, and feature updates don't need a human doing them manually; use AI to research and summarize new features so you can get up to speed on unfamiliar tech fast.
5. **Prepare for every meeting like it's the differentiator.** Bring a branded deck with cost consumption broken down by service, relevant new features, cost optimization strategies, and concrete progress against stated goals, pulling in a subject matter expert when the conversation needs one.
6. **Map your internal workflow and know who covers what.** Understand who approves, who unblocks, and who covers during PTO across legal, sales, technical account managers, bid desk, FinOps, and billing, then store documents centrally so anyone can answer a question fast.
7. **Prioritize customers deliberately, not reactively.** Rank by contract value, strategic importance, growth potential, and urgency, accounting for time zones across approvals and ticketing.
8. **Turn what works for one customer into a reusable template.** Share it with the team instead of hoarding it, and stay unified rather than each CSM solving the same problem separately.
9. **Overdeliver and lead with confidence.** Prepare enough that you never walk into a conversation unsure, and remember that the underlying goal is always the same: the customer trusts you with their investment.
